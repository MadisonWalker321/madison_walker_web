I am currently a SPARK Postbaccalaureate Fellow at the Harvard & Smithsonian Center for Astrophysics (CfA), where I investigate directly imaged planetary-mass companions in circumbinary systems using JWST observations under the supervision of Dr. Rachel Bowens-Rubin. My research focuses on exoplanet detection and characterization, particularly the formation and evolution of planets orbiting multiple-star systems and the implications of their architectures for planetary habitability.

My research employs high-contrast direct imaging, reference differential imaging (RDI), principal component analysis (PCA), angular and spectral differential imaging (ADI/SDI), point-spread function subtraction, and photometric analysis. I work with JWST MIRI observations and ground-based high-contrast imaging instruments, applying computational techniques to identify and characterize potential planetary companions.

Before joining the CfA, I graduated from the University of California, Santa Barbara in 2026 with a B.S. in Physics and a concentration in Astrophysics and conducted undergraduate research in the Mazin Lab, analyzing direct-imaging observations of HIP 38514 using MKID instrumentation, CHARIS, pyKLIP, and Orvara. I also worked as a Science Intern at Las Cumbres Observatory, where I analyzed the eclipsing binary asteroid system Didymos in support of ESA’s Hera follow-up to NASA’s DART mission.

In addition to my research, I have experience as a Learning Assistant for UC Santa Barbara’s physics department and enjoy teaching. I also have an active role in public outreach and the Boston community as one of the organizers for Astronomy on Tap. When I’m not looking for exoplanets, I enjoy playing water polo, managing a (somewhat) successful fantasy football team, and geeking out over all things Star Wars (including the planets in a galaxy far, far away…)
